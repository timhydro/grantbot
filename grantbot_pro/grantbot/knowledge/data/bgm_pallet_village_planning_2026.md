## 1. Project concept

Broken Growth Ministries is developing a small transitional-housing and reentry village concept for the property at **301 Cain Ave, Pensacola, Florida 32514**. The preferred concept is to replace RV/camper-based sleeping arrangements with code-compliant **Pallet-style modular shelters** and centralized supportive infrastructure.

### Program purpose

Primary mission populations:
- Adults returning from jail or prison who need stable reentry housing.
- Adults experiencing homelessness.
- Highest priority: people who meet both reentry and homelessness criteria.

Preferred village components:
- Individual climate-controlled Pallet-style sleeping shelters.
- Shared hygiene building with private toilet/shower rooms.
- Shared laundry.
- Shared/community kitchen or meal-support area if permitted.
- Case-management/program office.
- ADA-accessible route and required accessible facilities.
- Centralized water, sewer, electrical, lighting, fire/life-safety, trash, and security infrastructure.
- Employment, workforce, case-management, housing-navigation, and reentry services.

## 4. RV occupancy rule — VERIFIED
Escambia County Land Development Code Sec. 4-7.10 states that using an RV as living quarters for more than 14 days in a calendar year is prohibited outside a duly licensed campground, RV park, or mobile-home park except for specifically authorized situations. Short-term authorization applies to a single RV and requires it to remain highway-ready. Other exceptions have specific conditions.

Planning implication:
- Do not count the Silverback RV as a permanent residential bed in the village business model unless Escambia County expressly approves that use.
- The cleaner long-term development model is code-compliant Pallet/modular shelters rather than occupied RVs.

Source:
- https://myescambia.com/docs/default-source/sharepoint-developmental-services/zoning/land-use/land-use-certificate-rv-as-living-quarters_ver-2022_fillable.pdf

## 5. Local Pallet-shelter precedent — VERIFIED
The City of Pensacola's Pallet Shelter Program selected Re-Entry Alliance Pensacola (REAP) and another nonprofit partner for Pallet shelters for people experiencing homelessness. Pensacola reports that the shelters are climate-controlled and meet Florida building and wind codes.

Pensacola's 2025 Pallet Shelter Development Cheat Sheet states, for projects within City jurisdiction, that multiple Pallet shelters may be treated as dwelling units within a mixed-use or multifamily residential development; density must be reviewed to determine the allowed unit count. It also identifies requirements involving building permits, Florida-licensed contractors, structural engineering, anchoring, ADA access, electrical/plumbing/mechanical permits, building separation, fire/life safety, flood review, site engineering, water/sewer, and other site-specific requirements.

**Jurisdiction warning:** The City document expressly says properties outside City limits must contact Escambia County Development Services. The City standards are useful precedent and design guidance, but they are not automatically the governing code for an unincorporated Escambia County parcel.

Sources:
- https://www.cityofpensacola.com/3576/Pallet-Shelter-Program
- https://www.cityofpensacola.com/DocumentCenter/View/27602/Addendum-2-for-Pallet-Shelters---Deadline-Extension

## 6. Pensacola capital benchmark — VERIFIED PUBLIC FUNDING AMOUNT; NOT A PER-UNIT QUOTE
Pensacola set aside approximately **$1.011 million in ARPA funds** for the Pallet Shelter initiative. Public updates state that 27 Pallet shelters were ultimately purchased/allocated and that the funding supported Pallet shelters and related resources.

Simple benchmark calculation:
- $1,011,000 / 27 = approximately **$37,444 per shelter-equivalent of public funding**.

This figure must **not** be represented as Pallet PBC's current unit price. It is only a local public-program benchmark because the $1.011 million supported shelters and related resources and does not disclose a current standalone purchase price for each model.

The bathroom count is a planning target, not a final code determination.

## 10. Admission and bed-allocation business model

Preferred operating model:
- Mission-based transitional housing rather than ordinary public rental housing.
- Separate eligibility/funding lanes so the project can serve people who are homeless, returning from incarceration, or both.

Recommended 12-bed allocation concept:
- 6 dual-priority homelessness + reentry beds.
- 4 reentry beds.
- 2 flexible/bridge beds.

Core operating principles:
- Age 18+.
- Priority to people experiencing homelessness and people returning from incarceration.
- 30-day stabilization period before employment/training/job-search requirements.
- Individualized case plan after stabilization.
- Typical target stay of 6–12 months, subject to funding rules and reasonable accommodation.
- No applicant denied merely because they enter with no income.
- Criminal history alone is not an automatic denial; program-specific safety and legal restrictions remain applicable.
- Funding-program rules override internal preferences when a bed is supported by a restricted grant or voucher.
